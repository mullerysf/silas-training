Program Rules

Progressive Overload

When you hit the TOP of the rep range on ALL sets, add 5 lbs next session. Log every weight so you can track this.

Building Back Explosive Speed

Everything in the gym is full-intensity — heavy lifts, controlled tempo work, real leg strengthening. The one thing still building back is max-effort explosive movement: all-out sprints, jumps, and hard cutting on the golf course or tennis court. That comes back gradually with more reps of golf swings and tennis conditioning. If anything ever feels off in the hip, back off immediately.

Rest Between Sets

Big lifts (squat, press, row): 2–3 min. Accessory work: 60–90 sec. Supersets: move directly to the next exercise, then rest 90 sec.

Nutrition Basics

Hit ~0.85–1g protein per pound of bodyweight daily. Eat a meal or shake within 30 min after lifting. Don't train fasted — eat something beforehand. See the Tips tab for your full nutrition breakdown.

90/90 Hip Flexor Stretch
Sit with front leg at 90° and rear leg at 90° to the side. Tall spine, lean gently forward over front shin. Don't force it — breathe into the stretch.
How to do it:
  1. Sit on the floor. Front leg bent at 90° in front of you, shin parallel to your body.
  2. Rear leg bent at 90° out to the side, shin perpendicular to your body.
  3. Sit tall — don't round your back. Gently lean forward over your front shin.
  4. Breathe deeply into the stretch. Don't force it — let gravity do the work.
  5. Hold the full 2 minutes. It will deepen as you relax into it.
2 min/side

Kneeling Hip Flexor Stretch
Low lunge, rear knee on ground. Squeeze the glute of the rear leg — this is key. Shift forward until you feel the front of the hip. Hold still, don't rock.
How to do it:
  1. Take a low lunge position with rear knee on the floor (put a towel under it if needed).
  2. The key: squeeze the glute of your rear leg hard before leaning forward.
  3. Shift your hips forward until you feel a stretch at the front of the rear hip.
  4. Stay tall — don't lean your upper body forward.
  5. Hold still and breathe. The stretch is at the front of the hip, not the knee.
90 sec/side

Tennis
Thoracic Rotation (T-Spine)
Sit cross-legged or kneel. Place hand behind head, rotate elbow toward the ceiling as far as possible. T-spine rotation = serve power and forehand snap. Critical for tennis.
How to do it:
  1. Sit cross-legged on the floor, or kneel.
  2. Place one hand behind your head, elbow pointing out.
  3. Rotate that elbow up toward the ceiling as far as you can.
  4. Eyes follow the elbow. Hold 1-2 seconds at the top.
  5. Return slowly. The rotation should come from your upper back, not your lower back.
10 reps/side
✓
Tennis
Shoulder Sleeper Stretch
Lie on your side (dominant arm down). Arm at 90°, use other hand to gently press wrist toward the floor. Protects the rotator cuff from overuse injury — especially important for heavy serving.
How to do it:
  1. Lie on your side on the dominant arm (right side if right-handed).
  2. Your arm should be at 90° — upper arm pointing away from body, forearm pointing toward ceiling.
  3. With your other hand, gently press your wrist down toward the floor.
  4. You'll feel a stretch in the back of your shoulder.
  5. Don't force it. Hold and breathe. This protects your rotator cuff from serving stress.
2 min/side
✓
Tennis
Ankle Circles + Calf Stretch
10 slow ankle circles each direction, then calf stretch against a wall — straight leg and bent knee. Ankles take a beating with all the lateral movement in tennis.
How to do it:
  1. For circles: sit or stand, lift one foot off the floor. Draw 10 big slow circles each direction.
  2. For calf stretch (straight leg): stand facing a wall, hands on it. Step one foot back, press heel into floor.
  3. For calf stretch (bent knee): same position but bend the back knee slightly. Targets the deeper calf muscle.
  4. Hold each stretch 45-60 seconds per side.
  5. Don't bounce — hold still and breathe.
1 min/side

Pigeon Pose
From downward dog, bring one shin forward parallel to your hands and lower hips toward the floor. Deep hip opener — targets glute med and external rotators. Breathe and relax into it, don't push.
How to do it:
  1. From a push-up or downward dog position, bring your right shin forward and lay it on the floor.

Frog Stretch
On hands and knees, slide knees wide apart, toes out. Lower hips toward the floor. Deep inner hip/groin stretch — great for hip flexor recovery and squat depth.

World's Greatest Stretch
From lunge, place same-side hand inside foot on the floor. Rotate other arm to the sky, eyes following. Then drop elbow to the floor inside foot. Hits hips, thoracic spine, hamstrings, and shoulders all in one movement.
How to do it:
  1. Start in a lunge position — right foot forward, left knee on floor.
  2. Place your right hand inside your right foot on the floor.
  3. Rotate your left arm up toward the ceiling, eyes following it.
  4. Then drop that elbow to the floor inside your right foot.
  5. 5 slow reps per side. This hits hip flexors, thoracic spine, hamstrings, and shoulders all at once.
5 reps/side

Full Body
Hamstring Stretch (Standing or Lying)
Standing: hinge forward with soft knees and reach toward the floor, hold. Or lie on back and pull one leg toward you with a band or towel. Tennis players notoriously tight here.
How to do it:
  1. Standing: stand tall, hinge forward at hips with soft knees. Reach toward the floor.
  2. Hold when you feel tension in the back of your leg. Don't bounce.
  3. Lying version: lie on your back, loop a band or towel around one foot.
  4. Gently pull your leg toward you, keeping it as straight as comfortable.
  5. Hold 90 seconds per side. Breathe and let it relax deeper over time.
90 sec/side

Full Body
Cat-Cow
On hands and knees. Inhale — drop belly, lift head and tailbone (cow). Exhale — round spine to ceiling, tuck chin and pelvis (cat). Slow and deliberate. Keeps the spine loose and healthy.
How to do it:
  1. Start on hands and knees, wrists under shoulders, knees under hips.
  2. COW (inhale): let your belly drop toward the floor, lift your head and tailbone up.
  3. CAT (exhale): round your spine up toward the ceiling, tuck your chin and pelvis under.
  4. Move slowly between the two. Let your breath guide the movement.
  5. 10 slow, full reps. This keeps the spine healthy and mobile.

💪 The Mass Builder Smoothie

If you're struggling to gain weight, this is your secret weapon. Drink it in ADDITION to your meals — not instead of one. Best after a workout or before bed. ~800 calories and 45g+ protein of quality nutrition:

Whole milk (or Greek yogurt) 12 oz
Banana 1 whole
Peanut butter 2 tbsp
Rolled oats 1/2 cup
Protein powder (vanilla or chocolate) 1 scoop
Berries (fresh or frozen) handful
Honey (optional, for taste) 1 tsp
BLEND & DRINK ~800 cal · 45g protein

Why homemade beats the Smoothie King Hulk: same calories, but way more protein and way less sugar. This builds muscle, not just weight. Add more oats or PB to push calories higher. The key rule: it's an EXTRA on top of eating — if it fills you up and you skip a meal, it won't help you gain.

Carbs Are Your Fuel — Don't Skip Them

The easiest way to hit your calorie target is simple: add a starch to every single meal. Rice with lunch, oatmeal at breakfast, potato at dinner. Without enough carbs your body burns muscle for energy instead of building it — which defeats the whole point of lifting. You're not cutting weight, you're building it. Eat the carbs.

The Post-Workout Window

Within 30–45 minutes of finishing a lift, eat protein and carbs. Your muscles are primed to absorb nutrients right after training and this is when most of the actual muscle building happens. A protein shake with milk is the easiest option. Don't skip this — it's one of the highest-leverage habits in the whole program.

Eat Before You Train

Never lift, play tennis, or play golf on an empty stomach. A meal 1.5–2 hours before or a light snack 30–45 min before gives you the energy to actually perform. Banana + peanut butter, oatmeal, or eggs on toast all work well. If you're dragging through a workout, low fuel is usually the reason.

Hydration in Florida Summer Heat

You're doing tennis and golf outside in 90+ degree heat with high humidity — dehydration will kill your performance before you even notice it's happening. Drink water consistently all morning, not just when you're thirsty. On outdoor activity days aim for at least 16–20oz in the hour before you start and bring an electrolyte drink (Liquid IV, Gatorade) for anything over 60 minutes outside. Pure water alone isn't enough when you're sweating heavily for 2 hours.
